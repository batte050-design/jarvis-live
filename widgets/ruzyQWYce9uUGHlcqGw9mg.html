Three secondhand phones under $300 for work and driving

Prices are the used and refurbished market as of October 2026, so treat them as ranges, not fixed. The right pick turns on whether a full day on maps or a better camera matters more.

~$130-200 used

Samsung Galaxy A54 5G

6.4in Super AMOLED 120Hz, Exynos 1380, 6-8GB RAM, 128-256GB plus microSD, 5000 mAh, IP67.

Pros: biggest battery here, expandable storage, 120Hz screen, cheapest of the three. Cons: weakest chip and a camera behind the Pixel.

~$190-250 used

Google Pixel 7a

6.1in OLED 90Hz, Tensor G2, 8GB RAM, 128GB, 4385 mAh, IP67, wireless charging.

Pros: sharpest screen, best camera, longest software life (updates to May 2028). Cons: no microSD, battery only average for heavy map use.

~$165-250 used

Apple iPhone 12

6.1in OLED 60Hz, A14 Bionic, 4GB RAM, 64-256GB, 2815 mAh, MagSafe, IP68.

Pros: smooth iOS, strong resale, long support. Cons: smallest battery of the three for long drives, buy only on verified battery health, no microSD.

Best pick

Samsung Galaxy A54 5G. For work plus all-day driving navigation its 5000 mAh battery and microSD beat the Pixel's better camera, and it costs the least. If photos matter more to you than a whole day on maps, take the Pixel 7a instead.

Three habits that keep the battery healthy

Live in the 20-80% band

Top up in short bursts rather than running to 0 or sitting at 100%. Deep cycles and long holds at full charge wear a lithium cell fastest.

Don't baby it on the car charger

A phone that lives plugged in at 100% on a hot dashboard ages quickest. Let it drop, and keep it out of direct sun while navigating.

Slow charge overnight

Use a plain 10-15W charger overnight, keep fast charging for the day, and enable the battery-protection or optimised-charging setting so it stops at 85%.
