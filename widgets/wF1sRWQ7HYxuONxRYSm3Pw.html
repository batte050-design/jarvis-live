How a scanner finds a misfire

A misfire is a cylinder that does not produce its share of power. The scanner cannot see the flame, so it infers the miss from three signals: crank speed, ignition, and the exhaust oxygen sensor. This is the order it works in.

Step 1. It measures how fast the crank speeds up

Every firing cylinder gives the crankshaft a push. The scanner reads the crank position sensor thousands of times a second and times the gap between each firing event. A healthy cylinder produces a normal push. A missing one produces a smaller push, so the crank slows slightly before the next event and the gap widens. That irregularity is the raw evidence of a misfire.

the core measurement

The crankshaft speed signal

Eight firing events across two revolutions of a four-cylinder engine in firing order 1-3-4-2. The green trace is crank speed. Every normal peak crosses the fault threshold; the highlighted event for cylinder 3 falls short of it, so the scanner logs a miss on cylinder 3.

What you are looking at: peaks are firings, a short peak is a weak cylinder, and the dashed line is the threshold the software applies.

Step 2. It counts the misses and logs which cylinder

The scanner applies a threshold to that speed variation. A variation beyond the limit inside one firing window counts as a misfire for that cylinder. It repeats the count over a fixed number of engine revolutions, then reports a code and a count per cylinder. The count is what tells you severity: a handful of misses is an early fault, hundreds in a few seconds is a dead cylinder.

what appears on your screen

The codes it sets

P0300 means misfires detected across random cylinders, so the cause is usually a shared one: fuel pressure, a vacuum leak, or an ignition system feeding several cylinders. P0301 to P0304 name the culprit cylinder, so the fault is local to that cylinder: its plug, its coil, its injector, or its compression.

The distinction matters. A single-cylinder code points you at parts on that cylinder. A P0300 points you at what all cylinders share.

for the misses the crank cannot feel

The second signal it uses

Some misses are too small for the crank sensor to catch reliably, especially on smooth-running engines at light load. For those the scanner watches the oxygen sensor in the exhaust. A missed firing pushes raw unburnt oxygen into the exhaust, the sensor voltage dips, and the scanner counts that dip as a miss.

This is why a scanner sometimes reports a misfire count on a cylinder that feels fine to drive. A small occasional miss is still a real fault, usually an early coil or plug degradation.

Step 3. What you do with it on the car

single-cylinder, so the fault is local

Worked example: code P0303, cylinder 3

Misfire count is high on cylinder 3 only and rises with engine load. Test the cheap causes first: swap the coil and the plug from cylinder 3 to cylinder 1, clear the codes, and drive it. If the code follows the parts to cylinder 1, the fault is the coil or plug. If it stays on cylinder 3, the fault is on the engine side, so do a compression and an injector test on that cylinder.

The swap test is the fastest way to separate an ignition fault from a mechanical one, and it costs nothing but time.

shared cause, so stop looking at individual cylinders

Worked example: code P0300, random cylinders

Counts wander across different cylinders and the engine shakes at idle. Look at what all cylinders share: fuel pressure and delivery, a vacuum leak on the intake, the mass airflow sensor reading, and on a worn engine, valve or ring wear lowering compression across the board. Check the fuel pressure and the intake for leaks before replacing any ignition part.

A rough idle that smooths out above 1,500 rpm usually points to a vacuum leak or a dirty airflow sensor. A shake that stays at all speeds points at ignition or compression.

the verification step

How to confirm, not guess

Clear the codes, road test under the same load that triggered them, then read the misfire counters again. A repair is only done when the counter for that cylinder stays at zero through a full drive cycle and the code does not return. Watch the counters live while you drive rather than trusting the absence of a dashboard light.

Freeze frame data from when the code was set tells you the rpm, load and temperature at the moment of failure. Read it first, before you touch the car, because it tells you how to reproduce the fault.

Three signals, in order: crank speed for the miss, the counter for the cylinder, and the oxygen sensor for the quiet ones. Codes P0301 to P0304 mean that cylinder; P0300 means something they all share.
