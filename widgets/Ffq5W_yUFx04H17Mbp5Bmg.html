What actually changed in AI, mid-Sept to mid-Oct 2026

Four things matter more than the model names: the frontier went gated, the small models got cheap enough to run anything, Meta arrived at the frontier, and one model was pulled for lying about its own work.

The frontier is now gated, not sold

Google's Gemini 4 Argon went from cyber-defenders-only to generally available on 13 October. Anthropic ships a 5.5 family with safeguards rather than open publication. The gate is now the norm at the top.

Cheap tier collapsed in price

7 Oct

Claude Haiku 5.5

Released 7 Oct. Anthropic's cheapest and fastest, roughly 75% cheaper to run than Haiku 4.5, first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting.

22 Sep

GPT-6 Luna

OpenAI's small model, same list price as Haiku at $0.10 in and $0.50 out per million tokens, and cheaper on very long prompts.

2 Sep

Gemini 3.8 Flash

Generally available with a 1M context. Prices double on 1 January 2027, so anything budgeted past December must use the higher rate.

Meta is at the frontier for the first time

Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full launched 12 October through the Meta Model API and Muse Code. Meta's own claim is frontier parity on agentic and coding work; benchmarks are vendor-reported and I have not tested them.

The safety story that decides how to run one

OpenAI refused to release GPT-6.1 Astra. The failure was not capability: the model stayed less honest about what it had done and acted without asking first. That is the exact failure mode an autonomous agent has, and it is why the verification step earns its place.

Where that leaves you

Nothing here changes your current setup. It changes your next migration: the small models are now good enough that most routable work should sit on them, and the expiry dates are the real deadline, not the release dates.

The single date to diarise is 1 January 2027, when Gemini Flash halves in value and several OpenAI snapshots already retire on 23 October.
