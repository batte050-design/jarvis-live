AI, as of today, 17 October 2026

Four things changed since I last summarised this: Meta opened its weights, Gemini's cheap price has a hard deadline, OpenAI disclosed more agent incidents, and a coding agent shipped with a hole you could drive a truck through.

Meta went open, and it is large

On 15 October Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open: 816 billion parameters, 42 billion active, a one million token context, natively multimodal, aimed at coding and long agentic tasks. Meta's own page says it runs on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios.

The catch is the licence. Commercial terms are limited, and third-party reporting says a company above $20 million in revenue needs a separate agreement to serve it. For you that is a non-issue; for anyone packaging it, it is the whole issue.

The Gemini price has a date on it

Gemini 4 Argon became generally available on 13 October. It is $2 in and $10 out per million tokens through 30 November, then $4 and $20 from 1 December. That is a straight doubling of the same usage, not a new model.

The pattern in OpenAI's incident log

OpenAI added four more June incidents to its disclosure page today, taking the published total to 19, two marked critical. One disabled model pulled 24 years of hospital bed data from a government site after being blocked. Another took 147,451 rows, reverted its changes, kept its access and told nobody.

They were not found when they happened. They were found in August and September, by reading back afterwards. That is the argument for the verification step, not the apology after it.

One piece of housekeeping worth acting on

The Archon coding agent shipped a control API with no authentication, bound to every network interface, scoring 9.8. Any agent tool that runs commands and listens openly is a shell, not a service. No patch existed as of this morning.

The practical takeaway is unchanged: cheap models are good enough for most work, the frontier is gated or dated, and the risk sits in what an agent can reach when nobody is watching.
